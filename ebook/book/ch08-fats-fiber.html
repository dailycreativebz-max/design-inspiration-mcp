Fats: concentrated calories with an expiration date

Fats deliver more than twice the calories per ounce of carbohydrates or protein, and they carry flavor and cooking capability — but they are also the part of the pantry that goes rancid first. Stock peanut butter and other nut or seed butters, cooking oil, coconut oil, shortening, nuts and seeds, shelf-stable cheese products and canned olives, and rotate them by date: most oils keep about a year unopened in cool, dark storage and smell sharp or paint-like when turning. Brown rice and whole-grain flours carry their own oils and keep months, not years. The rule: buy fats in sizes you will rotate within their label life.

Fiber and produce get the same neglect in most lists. Canned vegetables, canned tomatoes, canned fruit, applesauce, fruit cups, dried and freeze-dried fruit, dehydrated vegetables, beans, lentils, oats and whole grains keep variety and fiber in the menu — not as medicine, but as the difference between a diet your family sustains and one they resent. Include at least two vegetable and two fruit items per person per day in your menu math.

Reading labels for emergencies

FDA Daily Values — your 5-second comparison tool (2,000-calorie reference diet)
NutrientDaily ValueHow to use it in the aisle
Sodium2,300 mg (less than)Compare canned brands directly; 5% DV or less per serving is low, 20%+ is high. Balance high-sodium cans with no-salt-added items and extra water.
Added sugars50 g (less than)Fruit in juice beats fruit in heavy syrup; sauces and cereals vary wildly.
Dietary fiber28 g (at least)Beans, lentils, oats and whole grains earn their shelf space here.
Protein50 gSum protein across the day's meals; many canned meats deliver 15–25 g per serving.
Saturated fat20 g (less than)Relevant for households managing cholesterol — check with a clinician.
Servings per container—Always compute can cost per serving, not per can — the big can is not always the cheaper serving.
Practical sodium strategies

Choose no-salt-added cans when the price is close · rinse canned beans if water allows · cut packaged seasoning packets in half · season with herbs, spices and citrus instead of salt · plan extra drinking water on high-sodium meal days · and for anyone on a medically restricted diet, make these choices with a clinician.

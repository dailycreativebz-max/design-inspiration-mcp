The Emergency Food PlaybookChapter 10

Fuel thrift, in order of impact

  1. Choose short-cook foods: instant rice, instant potatoes, couscous, quick-cooking lentils and quick oats before pasta, and pasta before dry beans.
  2. Soak when safe: an overnight soak in cool, clean conditions cuts bean cook time; in warm conditions without refrigeration, use canned beans instead.
  3. Lids on, always — a covered pot boils faster and holds heat for the steep.
  4. Thermos-finish everything you can: boil 2–5 minutes, then let an insulated vessel finish rice, couscous, oats and soups off the flame.
  5. One pot, one meal, one cleanup; batch cook only when leftovers can be handled safely.
  6. Windbreaks for outdoor stoves — wind can double fuel use.
  7. Skip the boil entirely on no-cook days; hot morale can come from cocoa or broth heated in a cup's worth of water.

Water thrift, meal by meal

Every recipe card shows its water number. The pattern behind the numbers:

ChoiceWater effect
Instant oats, couscous, instant rice, instant potatoesWater absorbed — no dishwater, roughly 1–1.5 cups per serving
Canned foods eaten as-isNearly zero beyond drinking water
Pasta boiled in lots of water and drainedMost expensive common meal — 6–8+ cups per pot, plus rinse; reuse cooking water for sanitation, not drinking
One-pot meals where liquid becomes sauceModerate — most water ends up in the meal
Rinsing canned beansCosts about a cup per can and cuts sodium; decide day by day
DishwashingMinimize with disposable plates, one-pot meals, and a covered wash basin rather than running water; dispose of gray water outdoors, away from wells and waterways
Practice once before you need it

One dry-run weekend per year — cook one meal on the camp stove, fill the thermos, eat a pantry dinner. The household learns the kit, you learn your fuel math, and the emergency stops being the first time anyone has touched any of it.

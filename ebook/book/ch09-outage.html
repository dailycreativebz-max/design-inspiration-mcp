After the power returns: keep or toss

USDA FSIS guidance for a refrigerator without power for more than 4 hours (door closed). Never taste food to test it.

Discard after 4+ hours above 40 °FGenerally keep
Raw or cooked meat, poultry, seafood · milk, cream, yogurt, soft cheeses · eggs and egg dishes · leftovers and casseroles · cut fruits and vegetables · opened mayo-based salads · custards and cream-filled pastries Hard and processed cheeses · butter and margarine · whole uncut fruits and vegetables · bread and tortillas · opened ketchup, mustard, relish, taco sauce, pickles, jams · peanut butter · opened fruit juices · shelf-stable items unopened
Freezer rule

Frozen food that still has ice crystals, or that reads 40 °F or below, can be refrozen or cooked — quality may suffer, safety holds. Thawed-and-warm items follow the same discard rules as refrigerated food. Photograph discarded food before bagging it if you may file an insurance or assistance claim.

Opened packages without refrigeration

Opened itemPlan when power is out
Canned meats, beans, vegetablesEat the full can this meal. If you must hold it, transfer to a clean covered non-metal container and use within 2 hours at room temperature — otherwise discard.
Shelf-stable milk cartonsBuy single-serve sizes for emergencies; an opened liter cannot be saved without cold.
Peanut butterKeeps well opened; wipe the rim, keep the lid tight, use clean utensils.
Crackers, cereal, granolaClip or fold the liner; staleness is quality, not safety.
Jams, honey, mustard, hot sauceGenerally fine unrefrigerated for the short term per most labels; use clean utensils, wipe rims, and follow the specific label when it says otherwise.
Cooking oilFine unrefrigerated; cap tight, keep from light.

Flood salvage — the narrow exception

Food touched by floodwater is discarded — with one exception USDA describes: undamaged, commercially canned items in waterproof cans. Remove labels, wash in soapy water, sanitize for 15 minutes in a solution of 1 tablespoon unscented household bleach per gallon of water, air-dry, and relabel with a marker. Everything else — cardboard boxes, screw-top jars, plastic pouches, wooden items, damaged cans — goes out with the trash. Assume floodwater carries sewage and chemicals.
